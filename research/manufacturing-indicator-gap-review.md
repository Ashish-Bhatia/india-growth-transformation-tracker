# Manufacturing Indicator Gap Review

Status: Research review, pre-acquisition
Sector: Manufacturing (`MFG`)
Date: 2026-09-07

## 1. Purpose

Review every unresolved Manufacturing baseline dimension against the current registered indicator set, identify authoritative candidate sources and candidate measures, and record definition, coverage, methodology and comparability risks before any new indicator registration or data acquisition.

This document does not register indicators, acquire data, establish causal relationships, evaluate policies or assign scores.

## 2. Governing gate

The governing frameworks require the Manufacturing baseline to examine output, growth, productivity, investment, employment, wages where available, exports, imports, market size, capacity, fiscal support, infrastructure, financial access, competitiveness, quality, inclusion, international position and resilience where material.

The current registry contains seven Manufacturing indicators covering manufacturing GVA growth, manufacturing GVA share, IIP manufacturing output, ASI GVA, ASI persons engaged, PLFS manufacturing worker share and UNIDO manufacturing value added. The current baseline specification identifies the remaining dimensions as research gaps, not approved new indicators.

## 4. Source and methodology findings

### 4.1 Productivity

ASI is the strongest immediate candidate for a directly observed manufacturing productivity diagnostic because its published material includes GVA per person engaged, output per person engaged and wages per worker. These measures are explicitly for ASI-covered registered organised manufacturing and therefore cannot represent total manufacturing productivity without qualification.

A broader labour-productivity measure using NAS manufacturing GVA divided by manufacturing employment remains conceptually useful, but the employment denominator must be defined and validated before registration. PLFS activity-status concepts and the 2025 survey-design change prevent an automatic seamless series.

### 4.2 Investment

MoSPI's National Accounts framework provides time-series GCF/GFCF by industry and GFCF by asset and institutional sector. This establishes a credible source family for manufacturing investment. The exact cross-classification needed to isolate private and public manufacturing investment must be verified from the current series before an indicator is defined.

ASI provides fixed-capital measures for registered factories. These are useful as an organised-manufacturing capital-stock diagnostic but should not be labelled private manufacturing investment without further methodological work.

MoSPI's private corporate CAPEX survey is relevant to investment intentions, but intentions are not realised investment. It therefore belongs in a leading-indicator layer unless a separate methodology establishes its use for investment measurement.

### 4.3 Trade

Department of Commerce TradeStat provides commodity- and country-level exports and imports, with the FTSPCC system providing data from January 2010. This is sufficient to investigate the preferred 2013-14 baseline, but the tracker must first define the mapping from HS commodities to the Manufacturing sector boundary. Trade data must not be treated as manufacturing data without this mapping.

### 4.4 Capacity

RBI's OBICUS is a strong candidate for manufacturing capacity utilisation. RBI states that the survey has been conducted quarterly since 2008 and collects installed-capacity and production information. The survey is company-based, so the indicator must remain explicitly survey-population based.

### 4.5 Financial access

RBI's sectoral deployment of bank credit provides long-run industry credit data and industry-wise detail. It is suitable for a manufacturing-finance diagnostic after the classification mapping is defined. It does not measure total manufacturing finance because firms also use equity, internal funds, NBFCs and other channels.

### 4.6 Technology and innovation

DST publishes recurring R&D statistics, including business-enterprise participation and historical series. IP India maintains annual reports and patent information utilities. These are credible source families for technology/innovation diagnostics, but the Manufacturing boundary and construct must be specified before registration.

### 4.7 MSMEs

MoSPI states that ASUSE covers unincorporated non-agricultural establishments, including manufacturing, while ASI broadly covers registered manufacturing establishments. This creates a useful population complement but also a comparability boundary. ASUSE should not be silently joined to ASI as one manufacturing-enterprise series.

### 4.8 Quality

BIS provides certification and conformity-assessment information, including product-specific compulsory certification. These records are useful for standards and regulatory-coverage research, but certification activity is not equivalent to an economy-wide manufacturing quality outcome. A general quality indicator should therefore remain deferred until a defensible outcome construct is identified.

## 5. Cross-cutting comparability controls

1. Manufacturing sector boundary must remain aligned with the sector definition and avoid mixing economic-activity classifications with product classifications without a documented bridge.
2. Current NAS and IIP 2022-23-base series must remain separate from superseded methodologies unless an approved bridge is established.
3. ASI remains registered-organised manufacturing only.
4. PLFS 2025 remains a methodology-break control and is not automatically comparable with earlier annual PLFS estimates.
5. ASUSE and ASI cover different enterprise populations and must not be merged as a single population series.
6. Trade indicators require HS classification mapping before they become Manufacturing indicators.
7. International comparisons require the approved dynamic peer basket and harmonised definitions before benchmarking or scoring.
8. Derived indicators must preserve the source vintages and transformations used in their construction.
9. Missing observations remain missing. No zero filling or unsupported imputation is permitted.
10. A candidate source does not imply indicator approval. Registration requires the full metadata and methodology gate.

## 6. Candidate registration queue, after the gate

Priority candidates for formal indicator-registration review, in this order:

1. Manufacturing capacity utilisation, RBI OBICUS.
2. Registered-organised manufacturing labour productivity, ASI GVA per person engaged.
3. Manufacturing investment, subject to verification of the current NAS GFCF-by-industry series and private/public classification.
4. Manufacturing exports and imports, after HS-to-Manufacturing mapping is defined.
5. Manufacturing-related industrial credit, RBI industry-wise credit, as a financial-access diagnostic.
6. Manufacturing FDI equity inflows, after DPIIT activity mapping is validated.
7. Manufacturing technology/R&D diagnostic, after DST industry classification is verified.
8. MSME manufacturing conditions, after ASUSE/NSS population and methodology mapping is resolved.
9. Manufacturing wage/earnings diagnostic, after ASI/PLFS concept alignment is resolved.
10. Competitiveness derived indicators, after peer-basket and trade classification methodology is approved.

These are candidates only. No registration is authorized by this document.

## 8. Gate conclusion

The indicator-gap review is complete at the research-identification stage.

No new indicator should be registered and no new dataset should be acquired solely on the basis of this review. The next step is to take the priority candidates through the formal indicator-registration workflow, beginning with definition, numerator/denominator, unit, frequency, geography, historical coverage, methodology, source mapping, provenance, comparability and update-rule verification.

No attribution, causal inference, policy-effectiveness evaluation, counterfactual analysis or scoring is introduced by this review.
